## §1. E10a — GL-65 item 2, and the correction it needs first

**Type: C, small. Do this before E10c.** The owner has to tap approvals *during*
the live night, and a tap that vanishes there costs the night.

### The decision as approved

`answerCallbackQuery` at **receipt** — as soon as the callback is resolved and
the admin check passes — rather than after `handle_decision` returns. Scoped as
an acknowledgement only: it does not move the hourly poll's work earlier and
does not touch the offset/cursor handling GL-45 tested clean.

### The correction, and it is load-bearing

**As scoped, item 2 cannot deliver "visible in seconds", and there is a good
chance it delivers nothing at all.** Two facts the decision doc did not put
together:

1. **The ack already exists.** `publish_primary_group._ack` (line 47) already
   calls `telegram_client.answer_callback_query` on every dispatched decision,
   and `telegram_client.answer_callback_query` (line 145) has existed since
   GL-45. Moving it earlier in the same cycle only removes `handle_decision`'s
   own runtime — seconds to a few minutes — from the latency.
2. **The dominant term is the cadence, not the dispatch.** The poll runs
   **hourly**. Tap-to-toast is therefore up to ~60 minutes whichever end of
   `process_update` the ack sits at. And Telegram rejects a stale
   `callback_query_id` (*"query is too old and response timeout expired or query
   ID is invalid"*) — the exact bound is undocumented, but it is minutes, not an
   hour. `_ack`'s own docstring says a stale query is "a lost spinner, not a lost
   decision", which is exactly right and also means **the ack we already ship is
   probably failing in production nearly every time.**

So the change that buys the ergonomics is **the poll cadence**, and the ack move
is the cheap rider that makes it useful.

**Free falsification, do it first, costs one grep:** after the next scheduled
hourly run, `grep "answer_callback_query failed" logs/hourly.log`. If the acks
are failing as expected, that is the evidence for the cadence change; if they
are landing, the expiry reasoning above is wrong and this section gets rewritten
rather than built. GL-62 shipped the log that makes this readable.

### Scope

1. **Ack at receipt** — call `_ack` immediately after `resolve_callback` +
   `is_admin` succeed, before `handle_decision`. Keep the existing post-dispatch
   `_ack`/`_mark_decided` behaviour: the receipt toast says "received", the
   keyboard collapse still says what was decided. `_ack` must stay
   never-raising.
2. **Poll cadence: hourly → every 5 minutes** (M, one Task Scheduler trigger
   edit, owner's machine). Checked, and none of it bites:
   - `getUpdates` is free and unmetered by anything we pay for; 12 calls/hour is
     nothing against Telegram's limits. **No Replicate, Anthropic, Gelato or
     Etsy call is added** — `run_hourly` only polls and retries
     `publish_failed` groups.
   - **Lock contention is already correct and already silent.** A run that
     collides with a batch or with a long `handle_decision` raises
     `lock.LockHeldError`, prints, and returns exit 2 — **no Telegram alert and
     no heartbeat row**, so a 12× cadence produces no new noise. The skipped
     poll is retried five minutes later instead of an hour later, which is
     strictly better than today.
   - `heartbeat_status.py` has **no hardcoded staleness window** (verified — it
     prints the last row and judges nothing), so a faster cadence cannot make it
     lie.
   - **`JOB_NAME = "hourly"` stays "hourly".** Renaming it churns the
     `heartbeats.job_name` values, `heartbeat_status.JOB_NAMES`, the log
     filename and the Task Scheduler task name for zero functional gain.
     Record it as a deliberate misnomer in the runbook; do not rename.
3. **A test** asserting the ack is sent before `handle_decision` is called
   (order, not just presence) and that an ack failure does not prevent the
   decision from being recorded.

**Explicitly not doing:** early dispatch, webhook migration, cursor changes,
retrospective log-diffing. If taps still vanish after this, the next decision
point is a different confirmation channel — record it, do not iterate.

**Done when:** the receipt ack ships with its ordering test; the trigger is
changed and one tap during E10c produces a toast within seconds; the runbook
records the cadence and the misnomer.

## §2. E10b — the backlog recovery run, and the prerequisite nobody recorded

**Type: M + a throwaway script. Candidates 77, 78, 79, 81.** Verified against
the live DB today:

| Candidate | Niche (raw) | Status | Primary group | Telegram message |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 77 | `botanical/minimalist nature illustration - holiday_peak` | `primary_review` | 87, `pending_review`, no decision | 328 |
| 78 | `… - diwali` | `primary_review` | 93, `pending_review`, no decision | 397 |
| 79 | `… - black_friday_cyber_monday` | `primary_review` | 88, `pending_review`, no decision | 339 |
| 81 | `… - new_year_refresh` | `primary_review` | 94, `pending_review`, no decision | 408 |

### The prerequisite

**All four groups already have a `group_messages` row** — `group_messages` ids
46, 47, 52, 53, all in the admin chat, all sent 2026-08-10. (The chat id is the
`TELEGRAM_ADMIN_CHAT_ID` and is treated as a credential — it is not written into
this repo; read it from `.env`.) Two consequences the
"just run the batches" framing misses:

1. **`run_digest_cycle` will not re-send them.** Its selection excludes
   `g.id IN (SELECT group_id FROM group_messages)`. So no new digest entry, and
   therefore **no `📝 Redo copy only` button** — those four messages were sent by
   pre-GL-56 code and carry the old single-row, three-button keyboard.
2. **Those four keyboards are still live.** A tap on `✏️ Edit` there regenerates
   the artwork — the exact loss GL-56 was built to prevent — and a tap on
   `✅ Approve` publishes the *old* bad copy. Collapse them before anything else.

### The procedure

`handle_decision` is directly callable; it does not need a tap. Confirmed:
`handle_decision(conn, candidate_id, group_id, "redraft", …)` clears
`critic_pass_attempts` / `listing_texts` / `group_messages`, parks the candidate
at `generating`, then runs `compliance_draft` → `critic_pass(copy_only=True)`.
**Because it clears `group_messages` itself, the next digest cycle re-sends the
entry with the new two-row keyboard.** That is the whole recovery.

1. **Back up the DB first** — same discipline as GL-58's M half
   (`db/qhoto.sqlite3.bak-2026-08-11-pre-e10b`).
2. **Collapse the four stale keyboards** via
   `telegram_client.edit_message_reply_markup(chat_id, message_id, None)` for
   message ids 328, 339, 397, 408. Clearing beats a `noop` label here: these
   messages are about to be superseded, not decided.
3. **Redraft, one candidate at a time**, via a throwaway script that calls
   `handle_decision(..., "redraft", decision_notes="E10b backlog recovery
   (GL-55/GL-56)")`. One at a time, and read the result before the next — this is
   the first production exercise of both new code paths.
4. **Assert the hard constraint, do not assume it:** for each candidate, record
   `base_image_*` and the sha256 of the artwork on disk **before and after**. The
   acceptance test asserts this; the run should observe it too, because the test
   proves the code and the run proves this data.
5. **Let the evening digest re-send.** Set `RESEARCH_MODE=consume-pending-only`
   for these runs so the batch does not pile new candidates on top
   (`docs/2026-08-11-e9-operability-runbook.md`), and **unset it afterwards** —
   an env var left set is the kind of thing that silently becomes the default.
6. **Then review the four new digest entries normally.** Approve/redraft/reject
   on their merits; this is not a rubber-stamp.

### What to watch

- **`sanitize_niche` on the real strings.** All four niches are prefixed
  `botanical/minimalist nature illustration - <event>`, so the sanitised niche
  keeps a real subject in every case; `holiday_peak` → `peak` is thin, as the
  hand-off said, but it sits behind that prefix rather than alone. **Read all
  four sanitised niches in the log before approving any copy** — if one reads as
  nonsense, that is a `SEASONAL_TERMS` finding worth a row, and it is one line to
  extend.
- **`check_seasonal_terms` firing at all.** If none of the four drafts trips the
  output-side check, that is expected (the input side already removed the
  vocabulary) — but it means the output check is still unproven live. Note it
  rather than claiming it.
- Copy drift class (c) from GL-53 — `printable` / digital-download wording on a
  physical product — is what `check_forbidden_terms` now blocks. Read the whole
  row, not just the seasonal field.

### Prepared 2026-08-11 (branch `e10a-ack-and-cadence`)

`scripts/e10b_backlog_recovery.py` — three commands, owner-run from the repo root:

    python scripts/e10b_backlog_recovery.py plan       # read-only, touches nothing
    python scripts/e10b_backlog_recovery.py collapse   # step 2, Telegram only
    python scripts/e10b_backlog_recovery.py redraft 77  # step 3, ONE candidate

`base_image_url` / `base_image_local_path` / `base_image_sha256` /
`base_replicate_prediction_id` plus the sha256 of the artwork on disk before and
after, and exits loud if any moved. It then prints the whole `listing_texts` row.

**The `sanitize_niche` watch item is already answered, for free and offline** —

## §3. E10c — the one live night

**Type: T, live, owner-supervised. Needs its own explicit "proceed".** This is
the row that closes GL-7 and GL-52 and it is the last gate item under our
control.

### Why one night covers everything

| Item | What the night proves | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| GL-7 DoD 1 | the stale-schema refusal, exercised against a deliberately stale DB copy | free, offline |
| GL-7 DoD 2 | injected failure → Telegram, now that seven loops actually raise (GL-46/54) | free |
| GL-7 DoD 3 | GL-36's 404 reconcile — **cannot fire in dry-run**, so this is the only place it is provable | needs live |
| GL-7 rider | v4.12's stall predicate, with the constant temporarily lowered — never by waiting 14 days | free |
| **GL-52** | one fresh Gelato create **from the repaired template**, verified by `gelato_template_check.py <product_id>` **and** an eye on the Design editor | rides along |
| GL-55/56 | first production run of the seasonal guard and the copy-redo path | rides along (E10b) |
| GL-57 | first listing published *with* ranks — featured image correct at birth | rides along |
| GL-65 item 2 | one real tap, one toast in seconds | rides along |

**The create that verifies GL-52 comes from E10b's recovered candidates.** Under
v4.12 the Gelato product is created once, when all three groups have reached a
terminal decision — so driving **one** recovered candidate all the way through
(primary approved, then its 5x7 and 10x24 entries decided) produces exactly the
fresh create GL-52 needs. **No candidate needs to be generated for this.** That
is the sequencing insight: E10b is not merely cleanup, it is E10c's fuel, and it
spends no Replicate money.

### Non-negotiables for the night

- **Verify by measurement, never by status code** (GL-22a). After the create:
  `python scripts/gelato_template_check.py <product_id>`, plus the Design editor
  by eye — **the aspect number is blind to GL-52's crop-within-rectangle defect
  by construction.** That is the entire reason the row is still open.
- **Run `python scripts/gelato_template_check.py` with no arguments first.** Any
  Gelato dashboard edit and the check are one action, not two (CLAUDE.md). The
  template has been hand-edited twice; assume `static_config.json` is stale until
  the check says otherwise.
- **Read `logs/batch.log` and `logs/hourly.log` during the run, not after.** This
  is the first night GL-62's logs exist. E9 spent real time guessing at process
  state; do not reproduce that with the logs sitting right there.
- **Candidate 42's draft (`4549960823`)** was kept as GL-36's negative control
  and was to be deleted after the reconcile run. Check its state before the night
  and delete it after, or the GL-11 email's statement stays untrue.
- **Abort condition:** any Gelato create that produces a second product for one
  candidate, or any listing that appears without our ranks, stops the night. Both
  are regressions of shipped, tested behaviour, and diagnosing them at 23:00 with
  live money moving is the worst available time.

### The scheduled tasks

All three are **Disabled**, and GL-58 was the reason. That reason is gone — the C
half shipped and groups 2 and 38 are marked `failed_abandoned`, so the
every-cycle re-fail and re-alert cannot recur. **Re-enabling is now a decision,
not a dependency.**

Recommendation: **re-enable the hourly poll (at the new 5-minute cadence)
immediately** — it spends nothing and it is what makes E10b's digests and E10c's
taps work. **Re-enable the two batch tasks only for the night of E10c, with
`RESEARCH_MODE=consume-pending-only`, and leave them enabled afterwards only if
the night passes.** A batch task that researches new candidates is the one thing
that turns a supervised night into an unsupervised one.

**Done when:** GL-7 has a verdict (not a pause), GL-52 is measured closed, and
the board's open set is GL-11 alone.

## §4. New rows from the owner's 08-11 observation

Two drafts still sit in the shop with Gelato's default mockups and pre-GL-53
copy: *"Dense Wildflower Meadow Botanical Print…"* and *"Mid-Century Modern
Botanical Poster…"* — the latter as **two** listings (primary + 5x7). Both are
good artwork. Filed as **GL-66** (recover these two) and **GL-67** (a general
migrate-to-current-standards path). Both post-launch, per the owner's own call.

**The scoping insight that decides GL-67's shape, recorded here so the session
does not rediscover it:** two listings for one design *is* the pre-v4.12 shape,
and GL-22a Q2 settled that **no API path adds a variant to an existing Gelato
product**. So "migrate" cannot be a patch for these designs — it is
**republish as one new v4.12 listing, then delete the old drafts.** That is cheap
precisely because they are drafts: no reviews, no favourites, no ranking history,
and GL-41's permanent-URL freeze does not bite on a listing that was never
public. **The same operation on a *published* listing would be a different and
much worse trade** — GL-67 must say which of the two it is doing.

GL-67's parts mostly exist already: `handle_decision(..., "redraft")` for the
copy (GL-56), the compositor and gallery for the mockups, ranked upload (GL-57),
and the create-or-reuse path. What does not exist is the entry point — "take
this artwork, skip generation, rebuild everything downstream" — which is one
script over shipped stages, not new machinery. **The hard constraint is a free
win again: no image generation, ever, on this path.**

Also still owed from GL-57, and now done: the owner's manual featured-image fix
across the four clean-mockup drafts. **That leaves the pre-GL-57 published
listings** — correcting an already-published gallery means deleting and
re-uploading all 13 images with ranks and rewriting
`product_images.etsy_listing_image_id`. Post-launch, and it belongs with GL-67
rather than on its own.
